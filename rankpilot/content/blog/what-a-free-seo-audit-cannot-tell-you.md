An automated audit is fast, free and covers the checks a machine can do
reliably. It is also not a strategy. Here is the honest boundary.

## What an audit does cover

These are objective, verifiable from a page source or a crawl:

- Indexability directives and robots rules
- Page titles, meta descriptions and canonical tags
- Heading structure and duplicate titles
- Broken internal links
- Image sizes and modern format usage
- Core Web Vitals field data
- Structured data syntax

## What it cannot cover

- Whether your content is actually good
- Whether your topic strategy is sound
- Whether you are competing for terms you can win
- Whether your internal links describe the relationships a reader needs

These need a human who knows your business. No tool can tell you that your
pricing page should be linked from three specific articles.

## Why this distinction matters

The most common failure mode is treating an audit score as a goal. Teams chase a
number from 72 to 95 and discover that traffic has not moved, because the
remaining findings were all cosmetic.

Treat the audit as the first half of the work. The report tells you which of
your pages have objective defects. Fix those, because they are cheap and
unambiguous. Everything after that is judgement.
